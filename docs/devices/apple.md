# AirPods and Beats

PairFree is growing into one app for earbuds of several brands, with the same screens for
each. AirPods support is new and has not been tried with PairFree yet: nobody on the project
owns a pair, so every model relies on reports from people who do.

## Which phones

This section is about AirPods. The Beats on Apple's chips (see the note under the table) use an ordinary serial
connection, which every Android version allows, so they need no particular phone.

AirPods keep their settings on a Bluetooth channel that Android's Bluetooth refused on most
phones until Android 17, which has Google's fix; the note under the table says why that is not
the whole story. Before Android 17, only these open it, as LibrePods found:
- Pixels on Android 16 QPR3 (with the latest Google Play system update);
- OnePlus, OPPO and realme phones on Android 16.

On any other phone PairFree does not try, since what a refused channel does to the AirPods'
sound there is not known: it says that Android 17 is needed. Root is never asked for and never
used. If your phone is not on the list but has the fix, an issue saying so helps.

## What it shows

PairFree lists AirPods and the Beats on Apple's chips among your paired devices when the phone
shows Apple's accessory service for them, or when "AirPods" is in the name. The AirPods report
their settings when PairFree connects, and PairFree shows only the ones they reported:
- **Home:** the battery (each earbud and the case, or one for headphones); noise control: off,
  noise cancelling, transparency and, on models that have it, Adaptive Audio with its noise
  reduction; Conversation Awareness; noise control with one AirPod in; Personalized Volume.
- **Sound:** a custom equalizer of low, mid and high, on the AirPods firmware that has one (Apple's newest
  beta firmware, as LibrePods found).
- **Controls:** press speed, press and hold duration, swipe to change volume and its length.
- **Device:** automatic ear detection, and Off as a listening mode for the stem.

### Beats on Apple's chips

Written from Apple's own Beats app for Android (no Beats has been tried with PairFree yet; a virtual run on an emulator only showed what
PairFree sends and shows). Powerbeats Pro and Pro 2, Powerbeats Fit, Fit Pro, Studio Buds and Buds +, Solo Buds, Solo Pro, Solo 4 and Studio
Pro use Apple's B2P protocol: PairFree reads the battery (one level for the whole product), and for each model what Apple's app lists for it:
noise control (off, noise cancelling, transparency), noise control with one earbud, automatic ear detection, the microphone and the in-case
tones. Powerbeats3, Solo3, BeatsX, Flex and Studio3 use the older BCD protocol: the Studio3's noise cancelling and the Flex's "answer calls
automatically". There is no equalizer: Apple's app has none for Beats. Not covered: the Beats Pill and Pill+ and Beats 360 speakers, button
modes, tone volume and the reset commands. See `docs/apple/B2P.md`.

Not yet: head gestures, renaming, hearing health and hearing aid settings,
transparency customisation, and anything that needs the phone to pose as an Apple device.
PairFree never asks the AirPods for their keys and never takes them over from your other
devices.

AirPods support is ported from [LibrePods](https://github.com/kavishdevar/librepods) by
LibrePods contributors (GPL-3.0-or-later), which controls AirPods from Android and Linux, and
checked against [MagicPodsCore](https://github.com/steam3d/MagicPodsCore) by Aleksandr Maslov
and Andrei Litvintsev (GPL-3.0), which also lists the Beats and AirPods Max models.
`docs/apple/PROTOCOL.md` describes the protocol.

## Help wanted

A device report from any model helps, and above all from Beats:
1. Connect the AirPods or Beats in PairFree.
2. Tap **Copy device report** on the Device tab.
3. Paste it into a new issue.

It lists the model number, firmware, battery and the settings the AirPods reported, with no
name, serial number or address. Say which phone and Android version you used, and which settings
worked.

<b>Why some of these need a particular Android version.</b> AirPods and AirPods Max take their settings over a classic Bluetooth L2CAP channel (PSM 0x1001). Android has no public call for a classic L2CAP channel (its public L2CAP calls are for Bluetooth LE, and the newest one accepts channel numbers 128 to 255 only), so an app can reach it only through <code>BluetoothSocket</code>'s hidden constructors, and only where the phone's Bluetooth stack accepts the channel. Until Android 17 the stack refused it except on Pixels with Android 16 QPR3 and on OnePlus, OPPO and realme phones with Android 16. Google fixed the stack in Android 17, but Android 17 builds can also hide those constructors from apps. Where they are hidden, PairFree asks Android's runtime, for its own process and for that one class only, to show them, as LibrePods does (no root, no Xposed); it does so only for AirPods and only after the constructors came back hidden, and the connection report says whether it was tried. This is a workaround for a restriction, not a public API: it has not yet been confirmed on an Android 17 phone, and a later Android version may close it. Apple's own Beats app on Android does not use that channel: it talks to the Beats on Apple's chips over an ordinary Bluetooth serial connection (RFCOMM), which any Android version allows. PairFree now speaks that protocol (B2P) to the Beats that Apple's app controls that way (Powerbeats Pro and Pro 2, Powerbeats Fit, Fit Pro, Studio Buds and Buds +, Solo Buds, Solo Pro, Solo 4, Studio Pro): they need no particular Android version. It shows the battery, the listening mode, one-bud noise cancelling, ear detection, the microphone and the in-case tones, for the models Apple's app lists each for, written from Apple's app and not yet tried on any Beats. The older Beats (Powerbeats3, Solo3, BeatsX, Flex, Studio3) use another protocol, BCD, over the same serial channel: PairFree shows their noise cancelling switch (and the Flex's auto answer), also untried; the Beats Pill+ speaker is not covered. See docs/apple/B2P.md.
