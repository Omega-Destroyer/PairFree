# Cambridge Audio

PairFree is growing into one app for earbuds of several brands, with the same screens for each.
Cambridge Audio support is new and has not been tried with PairFree on any earbuds yet: nobody on the project
owns a pair, so every model relies on reports from people who do. The official Melomania app was run against
virtual earbuds to check what it sends (see below), which is a separate thing from testing PairFree on real earbuds.

PairFree lists Melomania 1+, Melomania Touch and the Melomania Connect models (A100, A100 SE, M100/T100, P100, P100 SE, X100) among your paired devices by their Bluetooth name, and talks
to them over the same Bluetooth connection the Melomania app uses. Close the Melomania app first: only one app can
hold that connection.

What it shows, where the model has it:
- **Home:** the battery of each earbud; on the Touch, ambient sound and its level.
- **Sound:** six presets and five gain sliders from -6 to +6 dB, numbered, because the Melomania app does not name
  the bands' frequencies (it labels the ends Bass and Treble). Choose a preset before editing a curve whose values
  PairFree has not set. Edits use the earbuds' custom EQ mode.
- **Controls:** which built-in touch functions are on (play and pause, next, previous, volume, call answer, reject
  and end, voice assistant, and ambient sound on the Touch). PairFree changes the one bit you change and leaves the
  rest of the device's mask alone.
- **Device:** the eight voice guidance languages and the codec the earbuds offer (SBC, AAC, aptX, or AAC and aptX), when
  both earbuds are in range. The codec preference changes what the earbuds offer; Android chooses the one it plays.
- The device report keeps the answers PairFree asked for, with the two Bluetooth addresses blanked.

**Melomania Connect models** (read from Melomania Connect 2.6.0, which shows each model only the controls it has; Qualcomm GAIA v3 with Cambridge's own commands; no pair has been tried with PairFree): noise control on Home with the choice of modes the button cycles through; Sound has the equalizer (the app's presets for the model and a curve of your own, over the bands the earbuds report), left/right balance, and where the model has them gaming mode, mono audio, loudness, side tone and spatial audio; Controls has the touch gestures the earbuds list (pairing mode and wear detection among them) and three case and power gestures that can be switched but not read; Device has sleep mode, wear detection, the find-my-earbuds alert, the voice language, automatic power off (30 or 60 minutes, or never) and which codecs the earbuds offer. Rows show once the earbuds answer their read. Not built yet: the per-gesture actions of the assign screen and the list of connected devices (see the protocol document for what was checked); flight mode is only on the app's test page and is not sent.

Not covered: the original Melomania 1; the three custom EQ slots the Melomania app keeps on the phone; and the
high-performance/low-power audio switch, which the app does by installing a different firmware image, so PairFree
leaves it alone (it never sends firmware, a factory reset or a power-off).

## Tested virtually

Melomania 1+ and Touch have been connected to the **official Melomania app** as virtual Bluetooth devices. This checks
the app's outgoing command encoding against synthetic device replies; neither model counts as tried on real earbuds.
The [virtual-test record](../cambridge/VIRTUAL_TESTING.md) lists exactly which settings were exercised.

Cambridge Audio support is written from the Melomania app (2.8.12), read for its messages; none of its code is in
PairFree. [`docs/cambridge/PROTOCOL.md`](../cambridge/PROTOCOL.md) describes the protocol.

## Help wanted

A device report from either model helps:
1. Connect the earbuds in PairFree.
2. Tap **Copy device report** on the Device tab.
3. Paste it into a new issue.
