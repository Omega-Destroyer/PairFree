# JBL

PairFree is growing into one app for earbuds of several brands, with the same screens for
each. JBL support is new and has not been tried with PairFree on any headphones or earbuds
yet: nobody on the project owns a pair, so every model relies on reports from people who do.

PairFree lists JBL headphones and earbuds among your paired devices when their Bluetooth name
is one of the models below. Most of them take their settings over Bluetooth LE rather than the
classic connection the sound uses. PairFree first tries them at their own Bluetooth address;
JblTray found the Tune Flex 2 answers there. If a model does not, PairFree has to look for it
nearby, which Android allows only with the "Nearby devices" permission to scan. PairFree asks
for it only when you open a JBL model, uses it only for that search, and declares to Android
that it never uses it for your location. Without it, PairFree still tries the direct way.

What it shows, where the model has it:
- **Home:** the battery (each earbud and the case, or one for headphones), and noise control:
  noise cancelling, Ambient Aware and TalkThru, as JBL's app offers them on the model.
- **Device:** auto power-off (never, after 30, 60 or 120 minutes) and pause when taken off.

Some of JBL's newer models (protocol 4) connect through an extra security step. PairFree does
that step itself, so these are in the table: the Live 4 generation, Tune 530BT, 535BT, 680NC,
730BT, 735BT, 780NC and 785NC, Live 680NC and 780NC, Tour One M3, Sense Pro, Endurance Pace, Run
3 and Peak 4, Soundgear Clips, Junior Free and the Smart Tx. None has been tried, and the step
is read from JBL's app without a recording, so a report from any of them is especially useful.

Not yet: the equalizer, buttons, the noise cancelling level, spatial sound, voice prompts,
Smart Audio & Video, Find my buds and the other settings JBL's app has. Nor these models, which
JBL's app drives another way:
- Tour Pro 4, Tour One M4 and the newer Smart Tx, which JBL's app marks unsupported.
- Models built on Airoha's chips: Club Pro+, Reflect Mini NC, Reflect Aero, Live Free NC+,
  Live Free 2, Live Buds 3, Quantum TWS, Quantum TWS Air, Quantum 910, Endurance Race 2,
  Wave Beam 2, Vibe Beam 2, Tune 775NC and 777NC; and the LE Audio Tune 670NC, Tune 770NC
  and Tour Pro 3.
- Older models JBL's current app no longer describes: Live 220BT, 300TWS, 400BT, 500BT and
  650BTNC, Club 700BT and 950NC, Live Pro+, Tour Pro+, UA Project Rock, the Everest Elite
  range and Reflect Aware.

JBL support is written from JBL's own app (JBL Headphones 5.37.10), read for its commands and
for the settings it shows on each model, and checked against two open-source projects:
[JblTray](https://github.com/emperorbug46/JblTray) by emperorbug46 (MIT), which controls a
Tune Flex 2 from Windows and recorded the noise control commands from it, and
[JBL-Headphones-App](https://github.com/FajkPes/JBL-Headphones-App) by FajkPes, whose recorded
replies from a Tune 720BT PairFree uses as facts (it has no licence, so none of its code is
used). None of JBL's code is in PairFree. `docs/jbl/PROTOCOL.md` describes the protocol.

## Help wanted

A device report from any JBL model helps:
1. Connect the headphones or earbuds in PairFree.
2. Tap **Copy device report** on the Device tab.
3. Paste it into a new issue.

It lists the model, product id and firmware and the last answer to each read PairFree sends,
with the device's name and address blanked. Say which settings worked, whether PairFree had to
look for the earbuds nearby, and (for the newer models) whether it connected at all. A recording of JBL's app does not help yet: its settings
travel over Bluetooth LE, which PairFree's recorder does not read.
