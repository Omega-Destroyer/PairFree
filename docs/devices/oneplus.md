# OnePlus and OPPO

PairFree is growing into one app for earbuds of several brands, with the same screens for each. OnePlus and OPPO support is new and
has **not been tried with PairFree on any earbuds**: nobody on the project owns a pair, so every model relies on reports from people who do.
It is written from OnePlus's HeyMelody app (117.6.6, which also controls OPPO's earbuds), whose protocol and model catalog were read from the
app (`docs/oneplus/PROTOCOL.md`). Two kinds of virtual checks exist, both separate from real earbuds: the official app was run against a
virtual OnePlus Buds Pro 2 to record what it sends, and PairFree was run against a virtual one that answered as HeyMelody's parsers expect.

PairFree lists the earbuds among your paired devices by their Bluetooth name and talks to them over the serial service HeyMelody uses. Close
HeyMelody first: only one app can hold the connection.

What it shows, on the models whose catalog entry has each (Apple-style "show only what is reported" applies: a setting appears once the
earbuds answer its read):
- **Home:** the battery (left, right and case), noise control (off, noise cancelling, transparency) and, where the model has them, the level of the mode that is on (smart, strong, moderate, light; or the auto mode's range) and which modes a long press cycles through (at least two).
- **Sound:** the equalizer preset the model offers (the preset names are HeyMelody's English ones), and on the models with one the custom equalizers the earbuds keep: choose one, change its bands (-6 to +6 dB) and add a new one while there is room. Deleting and renaming one are left to HeyMelody.
- **Controls:** what a single, double and triple tap, a hold, a slide or a long press does on each earbud, and the call controls, among the functions the model allows (play or pause, previous, next, voice assistant, volume, switch device, game mode, nothing and the like). The slide, long press and call rows are written from the app's layouts and not yet checked against any reply.
- **Sound** also has spatial audio (off, fixed, head tracking) on the models that list it.
- **Sound** also has the switches for hearing enhancement, high-quality audio, automatic volume and the bass engine, on the models whose catalog lists them.
- **Controls** also has tap sensitivity (1 to 5) and, where the model has head gestures, a switch and which gesture answers a call (the other declines).
- **Device:** game mode, in-ear detection, dual connection (the switch, and a choice to connect or disconnect each other device the earbuds know; this phone is never offered), voice prompt volume, Swift Pair, sleep detection and the other switches the catalog gives the model, and a button that makes the earbuds play a sound so they can be found.

All of these are written from HeyMelody's code and only exercised against invented replies (no OnePlus or OPPO earbuds have been tried).

Not yet: the fit test and hearing tests (audible, phone-side), the bass engine's level, per-ear long-press cycles, personalized noise, zen mode and the
codec choices (`docs/oneplus/CATALOG_MAPPING.md` says what was checked for each). LE audio is switched by the phone's own Bluetooth stack, not by a setting.
Debug, firmware, find-my and account commands are never sent. Realme's earbuds are not covered: they need their own app and evidence.

## Help wanted

A device report from any model helps:
1. Connect the earbuds in PairFree.
2. Tap **Copy device report** on the Device tab.
3. Paste it into a new issue.

It lists the product id, the capability bits the earbuds reported and the answers PairFree asked for, with no name, serial number or address.
Say which phone and Android version you used and which settings worked.
