# Google Pixel Buds

PairFree is growing into one app for earbuds of several brands, with the same screens for
each. Pixel Buds support is new and has not been tried with PairFree on any earbuds yet: nobody
on the project owns a pair, so every model relies on reports from people who do.

PairFree lists Pixel Buds among your paired devices when the phone shows one of their control
services for them, or when "Pixel" is in the name. They report their model when PairFree
connects, and answer only for the settings they have.

What it shows, where the model has it:
- **Home:** the battery (each earbud and the case), noise control (off, noise cancelling,
  transparency) on the Pixel Buds Pro, Pro 2 and 2a, and adaptive noise control on the Pro 2.
- **Sound:** on the Pro, Pro 2 and 2a the equalizer: Google's presets (Default, Heavy bass, Light
  bass, Balanced, Vocal boost, Clarity), the curve you last saved in Google's app as Last saved,
  and a curve of your own over five bands from low bass to upper treble; volume EQ, conversation
  detection, mono audio and balance. On the Pixel Buds (2020) and A-Series bass boost, Adaptive
  Sound, and on the A-Series the bass level and loudness compensation; on the 2020 model attention
  alerts (a baby crying, a dog barking, sirens).
- **Controls:** touch controls on or off; on the Pro, Pro 2 and 2a what press and hold does on each
  earbud (noise control, the digital assistant or Live Translate, as Google's app offers), the
  noise modes it steps through, tap to mute during calls and head gestures.
- **Device:** in-ear detection, and on the Pro, Pro 2 and 2a multipoint, the case sounds, volume
  level notifications and noise control during Live Translate; on the 2020 model sharing detection.

Google's app sets the equalizer in half steps; PairFree's in whole ones, so a curve set in Google's
app shows rounded until you change it here. Live Translate and the digital assistant need Google's
own apps on the phone.

Not yet: finding the earbuds (it goes through Google's Fast Pair service), the ear tip seal check,
spatial audio and head tracking (Android's own settings, which only system apps may change), sleep
mode (it follows a Pixel Watch) and the experimental features. The 2020 model keeps two more touch
actions that Google's app no longer lets you set; PairFree leaves them as they are.

Pixel Buds support is written from Google's own app (Pixel Buds 1.0.990706425), read for its
messages, settings and per-model features, and checked against
[pbpctrl](https://github.com/qzed/pbpctrl) by Maximilian Luz (MIT or Apache-2.0), which controls the
Pixel Buds Pro from a computer and was used with one, and the findings of
[OpenControl for Pixel Buds Pro 2](https://github.com/tedsluis/opencontrolpixelbudspro2) by
tedsluis, from recordings of a Pixel Buds Pro 2. The Pixel Buds (2020) and A-Series speak an older
form of the same messages that no other project describes; it comes from Google's app alone. None
of Google's code is in PairFree. `docs/pixel/PROTOCOL.md` describes the protocol.

## Help wanted

A device report from any Pixel Buds model helps:
1. Connect the earbuds in PairFree.
2. Tap **Copy device report** on the Device tab.
3. Paste it into a new issue.

It lists the model and firmware and the last answer to each read PairFree sends, with no serial
number, address or name. Say which settings worked and which did nothing. A report from the
Pixel Buds (2020) or the A-Series helps most: nothing PairFree sends them has been seen on a real
pair yet.
