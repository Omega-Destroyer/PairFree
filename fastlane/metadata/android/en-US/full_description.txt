PairFree aims to be one app for the settings of nearly any earbuds and headphones. It replaces heavy, brand-specific apps with a light, fast one that talks to your earbuds directly over Bluetooth.

Why PairFree:

• No account and no internet: the app has no internet permission, so it cannot go online
• Nothing leaves your phone unless you share it yourself, such as a device report or an equalizer profile. No ads, no analytics, no crash reports
• One app for every brand: the same screens, instead of one vendor app each
• Few permissions: Bluetooth for earbuds you have already paired, and notifications for the optional status notification. For some LE-only models (JBL, QCY, Sennheiser), finding nearby devices, asked for only when you open one and never used for your location. No location, no contacts
• Small and quick: about 6 MB
• Open source (GPL-3.0), with every protocol documented and tested in its repository
• Your equalizer as files: save, export and share your own curves
• Translations: a language picker, and a new language is one file added by pull request (right-to-left languages are mirrored)

Supported brands (models; tried on):

• SOUNDPEATS: 88; H3, Air5 Pro+, C30, T3 Pro, TRUEFREE Clip C10
• Soundcore (Anker): 83; Space A40, AeroClip, Q20i, Sleep A20, P30i, R50i NC. With the open-source OpenSCQ30
• Samsung Galaxy Buds: 16, from the 2019 Buds to the Buds4 Pro
• Sony: 24, WH-1000XM2 to XM6; WF-1000XM5, WF-1000XM6
• Bose: 22, QC35 to QC Ultra 2, without Bose's cloud
• AirPods and Beats on Apple's chips: 30; AirPods Pro 3. AirPods need Android 17, or a Pixel on 16 QPR3, or a OnePlus, OPPO or realme phone on 16; the Beats need no particular version
• JBL: 112; Live Buds 3
• Nothing and CMF: 19; Nothing Ear
• Google Pixel Buds: 5; Pro
• QCY: 194; H3 Pro, T13 ANC2
• 1MORE: 39
• EarFun: 26
• Cambridge Audio: 8, the Melomania earbuds and headphones
• OnePlus and OPPO: 53; Buds 4. From HeyMelody
• Sennheiser: 28 earbuds and headphones, from the Accentum, Momentum and CX ranges to the older HD, PXC and IE models, none tried yet

Each is built from the brand's own app, checked against open-source projects and users' device reports.

What it controls, on the models that have each:

• Battery for each earbud and the case
• Noise control: off, noise cancelling, transparency or ambient sound, with their scenes and levels
• The equalizer: the brand's presets, your own curves and saved profiles
• Touch controls: what each tap and hold does on each earbud, and turning touch off
• Game mode, two devices at once, LDAC and LE Audio
• The ear tip fit test, a seal check with tones from the phone, find my earbuds, voice prompts and water drain
• A hearing test that turns what you hear into an equalizer curve, and an A/B sound finder that tunes a custom curve by choosing between two sounds
• Settings only one brand has, such as Sony's speak-to-chat, Bose's listening modes, JBL's TalkThru, Sennheiser's sidetone and Auracast, or the AirPods' Conversation Awareness
• A status notification with the battery and buttons for noise control and game mode
• A home screen widget with buttons that set or step through noise control choices and EQ profiles

PairFree shows only what your earbuds support. Models not marked as tried rely on the brand's own app and on your reports: the Device tab copies a device report you can send. Record from the official app reads, on your phone, what the brand's app sends your earbuds when you change a setting, so PairFree can learn to do the same; it keeps only those messages and masks the earbuds' address and serial number.

Close the brand's own app before using PairFree: only one app can control the earbuds at a time.
