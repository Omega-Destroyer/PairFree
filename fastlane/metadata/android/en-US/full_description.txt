PairFree aims to be one app for the settings of nearly any earbuds and headphones. It replaces heavy, brand-specific apps with a light, fast one that talks to your earbuds directly over Bluetooth.

Why PairFree:

• No account: nothing to sign up for or log in to
• No internet: the app has no internet permission, so it cannot go online
• Nothing leaves your phone unless you share it yourself, such as a device report or an equalizer profile. No ads, no analytics, no crash reports, no cloud backup of your settings
• One app to rule them all: the same screens for every brand, instead of one vendor app each
• Few permissions: Bluetooth, for earbuds you have already paired, and notifications for the optional status notification. For JBL and QCY models alone, finding nearby devices, asked for only when you open one and never used for your location. No location, no contacts
• Small and quick: about 3 MB, with no login and no server to wait for
• Open source (GPL-3.0), with every protocol documented and tested in its repository
• Your equalizer as files: save your own curves, and export and share them
• Translations: the app has a language picker, and a new language is one file added by pull request

Supported brands:

• SOUNDPEATS: 88 models, tried on the H3, Air5 Pro+, C30, T3 Pro and TRUEFREE Clip C10
• Soundcore (Anker): 67 models, tried on the Space A40, AeroClip, Q20i and Sleep A20. With the open-source OpenSCQ30
• Samsung Galaxy Buds: 16 models, from the 2019 Buds to the Buds4 Pro. From Samsung's plugin and the open-source GalaxyBudsClient
• Sony: 24 models, from the WH-1000XM2 to the WH-1000XM6, tried on the WF-1000XM5. From Sony's app, checked against Gadgetbridge
• Bose: 22 models, from the QC35 to the QC Ultra 2. From Bose's app and bosectl, without Bose's cloud
• AirPods and Beats on Apple's chips: 30 models, tried on the AirPods Pro 3, from LibrePods and Apple's Beats app. AirPods need Android 17, or a Pixel on 16 QPR3, or a OnePlus, OPPO or realme phone on 16; the Beats need no particular version
• JBL: 112 models, tried on the Live Buds 3. From JBL's app, checked against JblTray
• Nothing and CMF: 19 models. From Nothing X, checked against nada and nadamas
• Google Pixel Buds: 5 models, tried on the Pro. From Google's app, checked against pbpctrl
• QCY: 194 models, tried on the H3 Pro and T13 ANC2. From QCY's app, checked against OpenQCY-Desktop
• 1MORE: 39 models. From 1MORE's app
• EarFun: 26 models. From EarFun's app, checked against Gadgetbridge
• Cambridge Audio: 8 models, Melomania 1+ and Touch and the Melomania Connect earbuds. From the Melomania apps
• OnePlus and OPPO: 53 models, tried on the Buds 4. From HeyMelody

What it controls, on the models that have each:

• Battery for each earbud and the case
• Noise control: off, noise cancelling, transparency or ambient sound, with their scenes and levels
• The equalizer: the brand's presets, your own curves and saved profiles
• Touch controls: what each tap and hold does on each earbud, and turning touch off
• Game mode, two devices at once, LDAC and LE Audio
• The ear tip fit test on SOUNDPEATS, Galaxy Buds, Nothing and CMF, Pixel Buds, Soundcore's A3951 family, OnePlus and OPPO, and a seal check with tones from the phone for every other brand; find my earbuds, voice prompts and water drain
• A hearing test that turns what you hear into an equalizer curve, on every brand whose earbuds take a custom curve, plus Galaxy Buds' Adapt Sound and OnePlus and OPPO's Golden Sound tests
• An A/B sound finder: PairFree's own way to tune a custom curve by choosing which of two sounds you prefer
• Settings only one brand has, such as each Galaxy Bud's ambient sound level, voice detect, Sony's speak-to-chat and DSEE upscaling, Bose's listening modes, JBL's TalkThru, or the AirPods' Conversation Awareness
• A status notification with the battery and buttons for noise control and game mode, in place of Google's Fast Pair notification if you like
• A home screen widget, 1x1 to 4x2, with buttons that set or step through noise control choices and EQ profiles for whichever of your earbuds is connected

PairFree shows only what your earbuds support. Models not marked as tried rely on the brand's own app and on your reports: the Device tab copies a device report you can send. Record from the official app reads, on your phone, what the brand's app sends your earbuds when you change a setting, so PairFree can learn to do the same; it keeps only those messages and masks the earbuds' address and serial number.

Close the brand's own app before using PairFree: only one app can control the earbuds at a time.
